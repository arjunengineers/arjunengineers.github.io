A mechanical foundation and a systems perspective

Background

My path into industrial engineering began with mechanical systems: understanding how components interact, how manufacturing decisions affect quality, and how analysis can support better design. Through production and quality assurance internships, I worked with process coordination, inspection, and manufacturing improvement.

At Clemson, I am broadening that foundation through human factors, optimization, systems modeling, and quality improvement. My projects connect these interests across different scales: an individual using an information portal, a community accessing public parks, a delivery fleet serving clinics, and a rotor assembly responding to modeled loads.

I am most interested in work that connects technical rigor with the people who use, operate, or depend on a system. I want to understand the problem clearly, choose methods that fit it, and explain both the result and its limitations.

How I approach a problem

  1. Define the decision. Clarify who the system serves and what needs to improve.
  2. Examine the evidence. Understand the available data, observations, and constraints.
  3. Model or prototype. Choose an approach that makes the important tradeoffs visible.
  4. Evaluate and communicate. Test the result, document limitations, and identify the next useful step.
