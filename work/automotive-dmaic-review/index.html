DMAIC Case Study — Automotive Manufacturing Safety and Lean Flow

Evaluating the evidence behind process improvement.

A comparative analysis of two published automotive manufacturing cases, focused on the strength of their measurement, root-cause analysis, and control plans.

Context
Graduate academic research project, Quality and Process Improvement, February 2026
My role
Author and Comparative Research Analyst

01The research question

How do two automotive-manufacturing case studies apply the same DMAIC framework to different problems, and how convincingly do they demonstrate that the improvements will last?

02My analysis

I compared a workplace-safety case attributed to Knop (2022) with a Lean manufacturing case attributed to Rifqi and colleagues (2021). I examined how each study defined its problem, measured performance, verified causes, selected interventions, and controlled the improved process.

The review considered SIPOC, Pareto analysis, Ishikawa diagrams, 5 Whys, SPC, process mapping, Kanban, Heijunka, and takt-time analysis. I assessed the reported improvements in production time, inventory, floor space, safety, and cost while separating measured outcomes from projections.

03Comparative framework

DMAIC phaseWorkplace-safety lensLean-flow lens
DefineInjury mechanisms and accident costMaterial flow, inventory, and production stability
MeasureIncident categories and measurement consistencyLead time, movement, storage, and takt time
AnalyzeVerified causes and human factorsWaste sources and production-demand mismatch
ImproveEngineering controls and risk reviewPull flow, production leveling, and replenishment
ControlMonitoring and sustained safety performanceAuditability and longitudinal flow measures

04Critical findings and proposed improvements

My analysis highlighted gaps in measurement-system validity, pre-implementation risk assessment, longitudinal control evidence, and the treatment of human factors. I proposed FMEA and HFACS-informed analysis for stronger risk understanding, IoT monitoring for more timely safety signals, and electronic Kanban integrated with ERP for improved production-flow visibility.

05What I learned

The presence of a DMAIC framework does not by itself establish the strength of an improvement claim. The quality of the measurement system, causal evidence, and control plan determines how confidently a result can be interpreted and transferred to another setting.

Scope note

This is my analysis of published cases, not a manufacturing intervention I implemented. The source papers’ operational gains are not my personal results. FMEA, HFACS, IoT monitoring, eKanban, and ERP integration were proposed enhancements, not deployed systems.
