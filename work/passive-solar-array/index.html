Passively Actuated Deployable Solar Array Using Shape-Memory Alloys

Exploring compact deployment through material behavior and folding geometry.

A solar-array deployment concept combining Nitinol shape-memory alloy with origami-inspired folding to explore compact storage and passive actuation.

IDesign intent

Spacecraft solar arrays must balance deployed area with tight stowed-volume and mass constraints. I explored a passive deployment concept that combines Nitinol shape-memory alloy with origami-inspired folding geometry.

IIMy contribution

I designed the concept around material-driven deployment rather than motor-driven actuation and examined fold geometry for compact storage. The work connected mechanical layout, material behavior, and payload constraints within one systems-design problem.

IIIEngineering focus

  • Passive actuation as an alternative to a motor-driven deployment architecture.
  • Origami-inspired geometry for compact stowage.
  • Integration of material behavior and mechanical layout.
  • Evaluation of the tradeoff between packaging and deployment needs.

IVReflection

The concept reinforced the value of treating actuation, structure, and packaging as an integrated design problem. A useful next step is a traceable mass and volume comparison, followed by deployment testing under explicitly defined conditions.

Scope note

This portfolio presents the concept qualitatively. Quantitative performance and deployment reliability are outside the scope presented here. No flight-qualified hardware is claimed.
